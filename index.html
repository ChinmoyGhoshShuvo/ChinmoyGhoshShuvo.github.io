03 Portfolio projects

V-01 Personal project · 2026 · Individual

Chattogram District April Heat Surface, 2015–2025 (3D LST)

I built yearly 30 m April land surface temperature composites for Chattogram District from 91 Landsat 8/9 scenes and turned them into an animated 3D heat surface in QGIS. The city core, the port/EPZ belt and the airport are the hottest ground every year. The district median swings between 33.0 °C and 37.3 °C with pre-monsoon weather, with no warming trend.

Individual · 2026 · QGIS 3D & PyQGIS · Python (GDAL, rasterio) · FFmpeg

V-02 Personal project · 2026 · Individual

Canada's River Pulse 2025

I animated every mapped river in Canada for every day of 2025 as 3D walls whose height is daily discharge and whose colour is estimated water temperature. The animation combines HydroRIVERS, GloFAS and NASA POWER data, rendered with PyQGIS. Total flow to the sea swings 5.7-fold, from about 39,100 m³/s in February to about 221,100 m³/s at the end of June.

Individual · 2026 · PyQGIS · Python (NumPy) · FFmpeg

Design after Aaron J. Becker's South Asia's River Pulse.

Surface Urban Heat Islands in Two Coastal Megacities: Chittagong vs Karachi

Data
MODIS land surface temperature, land cover, sea-level-rise exposure, 2000–2024
Method

Status

An automated Google Earth Engine workflow tracks surface urban heat island intensity from 2000 to 2024 in a humid (Chittagong) and an arid (Karachi) coastal megacity. R-03

Ranking Renewable Energy Sources for Bangladesh (AHP)

Study area
Bangladesh
Data
Survey: 188 public and 21 expert responses
Method
Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP)
Type
Bachelor's thesis · 2024 · Individual

For my bachelor's thesis at KUET, I combined a survey of the public and sector experts with an Analytic Hierarchy Process that scored solar, biomass, wind and tidal energy on maturity, reliability, cost and sustainability. Solar ranked first (priority 0.481) and stayed first in a 2026 re-check of the AHP matrices. The survey (188 public and 21 expert responses) found that financial constraints were the most-cited barrier to adoption.

R-04

Groundwater Potential Mapping of Bangladesh (Random Forest vs ANN)

Study area
Bangladesh
Data
19 factor layers · 2,144 sample points
Method
Random Forest vs Artificial Neural Network
Status
Manuscript ready

This project mapped national groundwater potential from 19 topographic, climatic, socio-economic and geological factors, using 2,144 sample points to train Random Forest and neural-network models. Random Forest was the more accurate model (AUC 0.97 vs 0.91). Its map shows 35.73% of the country with high or very high potential, while the hill districts have the lowest potential.

05 Academic projects

P-01 Studio project

Location Theories vs Reality in Dhaka Division

This project tested classic location theories against upazila population patterns in Dhaka Division using hotspot analysis, Local Moran's I clustering and pattern mapping. Narayanganj Sadar, Savar and Gazipur Sadar emerged as 99%-confidence hotspots around textile hubs, with no regular geometric hierarchy around them. The pattern fits growth pole theory rather than central place theory.

P-02 Studio project

Barishal to Padma Bridge Route Selection (GIS Network Analysis + AHP)

Using ArcGIS network analysis, this project compared the existing, shortest and a potential route from Barishal to the Padma Bridge link road by length, time, cost and road class. It then ranked the three with a five-factor AHP (CR 5.4%). The existing route scored highest (0.768) because it is the only one built mainly on trunk roads.

P-03 Studio project (URP 2212)

Satellite Town Site Suitability, Khulna (KDA Planning Area)

In a 7-member studio team, we screened 10 candidate sites near Khulna and ranked 5 of them with a weighted multi-factor analysis of 18 sub-factors, including accessibility, flood level, salinity and services. Site 9, beside the Khulna City Bypass, ranked first (6.54 out of 10). We then produced a detailed site analysis and a 167-acre land budget for about 58,450 residents.

P-08 Studio project (URP 4142)

Ecosystem Services and Disservices in Khulna City (Wards 17–19)

We surveyed 60 high- and low-income households in three Khulna wards to see who benefits from urban ecosystem services and who bears the disservices. Ward 18 benefits most overall, while Ward 19 benefits most from regulating services but is hit hardest by disservices such as waterlogging and mosquitoes. Most residents see the benefits as unevenly shared and do not know of any environmental protection law.

Group (6 members) · Questionnaire survey · Excel · Field photography

P-09 Studio project (URP 4142)

Carbon Footprint Mapping of Wards 17–19, Khulna City

We surveyed 108 samples in three Khulna wards and converted fuels, electricity, water, construction materials and road surfaces into CO₂ equivalents with DEFRA 2022 factors. Road circulation produces about 78% of the emission, Ward 17 has the highest settlement emissions, and almost all housing emission comes from construction rather than daily use. Satellite land-cover maps for 2019–2022 show built-up land replacing barren land and vegetation.

Group (6 members) · Excel · GIS · Python
